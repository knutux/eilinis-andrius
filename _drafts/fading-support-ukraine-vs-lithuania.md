In this future post, I want to explore how public and media interest in Ukraine’s war effort has slowly faded, despite the conflict being far from over. I'll draw parallels with how Lithuania, too, was once left to suffer in silence — during occupations, deportations, and decades of exile from Western consciousness.

The goal is not to accuse or moralize, but to reflect: why does sustained attention fade? What makes suffering visible, and what makes it inconvenient to remember?

I also want to raise the question: if we forget Ukraine now, are we repeating the same silence others once showed us?

This piece will be more serious in tone, perhaps with historical references and some personal context of how I see this shift happening today.
