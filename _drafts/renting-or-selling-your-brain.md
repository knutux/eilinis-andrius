This post will explore the uneasy thought that working in knowledge-based fields — engineering, science, writing — might not be just about _renting out_ our brains for 40 hours a week, but gradually _selling them_.

The idea builds on neuroplasticity: how long-term mental habits, fatigue, and role expectations shape our minds to fit narrow professional tasks, often leaving little energy for thinking beyond them.

It's a personal provocation, not a fully formed theory — a way of questioning whether our brains are being trained into intellectual specialization so deeply that we lose the space to dream, reflect, or shift direction.

The tone will be speculative, slightly ironic, and open-ended — meant to raise questions, not deliver answers.
