Every country that successfully markets itself abroad seems to have _a story_. A theme. A phrase or idea that can be told in one sentence, even if it’s oversimplified. Switzerland sells neutrality and precision. Japan sells culture and innovation. Estonia? E-governance.

What about Lithuania?

We’re not a tax haven. We’re not a geopolitically safe zone. We’re not famous for natural beauty, and we don’t offer a wildly unique tourism experience. Our economy is stable but small. Our tech scene is growing but not dominant. We don’t have oil, gas, or gold. We’re not even the cheapest destination in the region anymore.

And yet — if we could pick a story, could we still build one?

This post will explore two speculative, perhaps utopian, ideas that could give Lithuania a forward-facing identity.

---

**1. Made in CO₂ Negative Lithuania**  
What if we embraced sustainability not just as a goal, but as a brand? Lithuania has forests, wind, modest scale, and a tech-savvy culture — we could aim to be the first nation to go beyond carbon neutral and build an identity around _negative emissions production_. Food, fiber, wood, digital services — stamped with “Made in CO₂ Negative Lithuania.”

It’s ambitious, but it’s a differentiator. And it fits a country that’s small enough to be nimble.

---

**2. LAIBA — Lithuanian Artificial Intelligence-Based Agriculture**  
A much more utopian vision: what if Lithuania became the global hub for AI-assisted farming innovation?

It ties into our roots — literally. We are still a nation with rural memory. Agriculture is deep in our culture. Combine that with world-class sensors, predictive modeling, and autonomous systems, and Lithuania could brand itself as the testbed for future farming.

“LAIBA” may be imaginary for now — but it sounds like something a country with ambition might try.

---

This post isn’t a policy pitch. It’s a challenge. If we don’t tell our story, someone else will — or worse, no one will bother at all.
