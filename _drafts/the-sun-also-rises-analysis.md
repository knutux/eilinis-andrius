This post will offer a quiet, personal reflection on _The Sun Also Rises_. Not a formal literary analysis, but an attempt to articulate what lingers after the last page: the motion without change, the emptiness between events, and the strange comfort of circular stories.

I want to explore how Hemingway captures meaning not in what is said, but in what isn’t — and how the book’s silence feels emotionally louder than most novels.

The post may also touch on how it resonated with me personally, and whether it reflects something deeper about burnout, masculinity, or the longing for life to move but never arrive.

The tone will be meditative and open to interpretation — more feeling than argument.
