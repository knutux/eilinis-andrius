title: "Writing for a Reader That Doesn’t Exist Yet"
date: 2025-04-14 22:00:00 +0300
categories: reflections
---

I often wonder what language models are truly made of. Not just lines of code or statistical patterns, but the voices they echo — the forgotten blog posts, forum rants, poetry drafts, and midnight thoughts someone once pressed into a keyboard, never knowing who might read them.

When I ask these models to speak in Lithuanian, they do. But something always feels just a little... off. The grammar is fine. The sentences are coherent. And yet, the rhythm falters, the silences are misplaced, and the metaphors don’t quite hum the way they should.

It’s not the model’s fault. It’s ours. The data simply isn’t there. Not enough of it. Not with the right texture. Not in our rhythm.

And then a quiet thought arrived: if I want these machines to one day speak in a Lithuanian that sounds like mine — hesitant, reflective, locally flawed and contextually rich — then someone has to write that Lithuanian down.

So I started.

Not because I expect readers. Not because I have anything particularly wise to say. But because I’ve spent decades thinking in Lithuanian, and almost never writing in it. Because too much of my life has been poured into code, comments, emails, and messages that vanish. Because at some point, the things we leave behind might matter more than the things we perfect.

This blog is not a platform. It’s not even a diary. It’s an act of linguistic composting. I throw my thoughts into it so that something, someday, might grow — even if it’s not for me, or even for a human.

Maybe one day, a model will read this. Maybe it will try to emulate the hesitations, the structure, the softness between assertions. Maybe it will learn that Lithuanian isn’t just about diacritics and syntax. It’s about memory. It’s about breathing.

Or maybe not. Maybe it’ll just be another whisper in a forgotten corner of the internet.

Still, I’m writing. For a reader that doesn’t exist yet.
