This post will explore the idea of social life as a kind of ongoing performance — a circus we all participate in, whether consciously or not. The metaphor of the _social circus_ connects different periods of history: the elaborate etiquette of Renaissance courts, the performative Sunday visits of the 19th century, and today’s curated identities on Instagram, TikTok, and LinkedIn.

Despite the changing forms, the essence remains: we dress up, rehearse lines, compete for attention, and fear exclusion. Even the clowns have roles — sometimes us, sometimes the algorithms.

This piece will be light, slightly satirical, but rooted in observation and history. It might also include moments of personal reflection: how it feels to watch the circus and not always want to join in.
